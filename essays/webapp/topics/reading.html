Reading

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Some people think books are losing importance as a source of information and entertainment. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that books are becoming less important as sources of information and entertainment. I partly agree with this view, since digital media has clearly taken over many of the functions books once served, although books still hold a valuable place in society.

One reason I agree with this statement is that most people now turn to the internet for information rather than books. Websites, videos, and articles provide instant access to up-to-date knowledge on almost any topic, which printed books simply cannot match, since they can quickly become outdated. Similarly, entertainment has shifted significantly towards streaming services, video games, and social media, which offer more immediate and interactive experiences than reading. For example, many young people now prefer watching a film adaptation or browsing short videos rather than reading a novel that covers the same story.

However, I do not believe books have lost their importance completely. Reading still offers unique benefits that digital media cannot fully replace, such as improved concentration, deeper understanding, and reduced screen fatigue. Many people also continue to value the experience of reading a physical book, particularly for relaxation or immersive storytelling. Additionally, books remain essential in education, as textbooks and academic literature still provide reliable, well-structured information that supports serious study.

In conclusion, while digital media has clearly reduced the everyday role of books for quick information and entertainment, I do not believe books have lost their importance altogether. They continue to offer unique value, particularly in education and deep, focused reading.

Two-Part Question

Children who start reading earlier in life perform better later on in their school studies. How important are early reading skills in a child's academic performance? What other preschool factors influence a child's academic success?

Some people believe that children who begin reading at a young age tend to perform better academically later in life. This essay will discuss the importance of early reading skills and identify other preschool factors that also contribute to academic success.

Early reading skills play a significant role in a child's later academic performance. Children who learn to read early often develop stronger vocabulary, comprehension, and concentration skills, which benefit them across nearly all school subjects, not just language-based ones. Reading from a young age also helps children become familiar with sentence structure and grammar naturally, making it easier for them to express ideas clearly in writing later on. For example, children regularly read to by parents before starting school often show stronger literacy skills once they begin formal education.

However, early reading is not the only factor influencing academic success. A child's early social and emotional development is equally important, since children who learn to interact well with others, share, and manage their emotions often adapt more easily to the school environment. In addition, early exposure to numeracy skills, such as counting and basic problem-solving, helps prepare children for mathematics later in their education. A stable, supportive home environment, where parents are actively involved in a child's early learning, also plays a crucial role in shaping long-term academic achievement.

In conclusion, while early reading skills are clearly important for later academic success, other factors, such as emotional development, early numeracy, and parental involvement, are equally significant in preparing children for school.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Some people think that children who spend a lot of time reading children's story books are wasting their time which could be better used doing other more useful activities. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that children who spend a great deal of time reading storybooks are wasting time that could be spent on more practical activities. I disagree with this view, as I believe reading storybooks provides valuable benefits that are just as important as other activities.

One reason I disagree with this opinion is that reading storybooks significantly supports a child's language development. Regular reading helps children build vocabulary, improve grammar, and develop stronger comprehension skills, all of which are essential for academic success across every subject, not only English or literature. In addition, storybooks often introduce children to new ideas, emotions, and imaginative scenarios, helping them develop empathy and creativity. For example, children who read stories involving different characters and situations often become better at understanding other people's perspectives and emotions.

While it is true that children also need to engage in physical activities, social interaction, and other forms of learning, reading should not be viewed as time wasted in comparison. A well-rounded childhood should include a variety of activities, and reading is one of the most valuable, given its long-term benefits for literacy and cognitive development. Rather than replacing reading with other activities entirely, children would benefit most from balancing reading with sufficient exercise, social time, and outdoor play.

In conclusion, I disagree that reading storybooks is a waste of children's time. Instead, it offers essential developmental benefits and should be considered a valuable part of a balanced childhood, alongside other important activities.

Discussion

Some people think that e-books are the death of paper books, while others think that paper books will never disappear. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.

There is ongoing debate about whether e-books will eventually replace paper books completely, or whether printed books will always remain popular. This essay will discuss both views before presenting my own opinion.

Those who believe e-books will replace paper books point to the growing convenience and popularity of digital reading. E-books are often cheaper, more portable, and instantly accessible, allowing readers to carry hundreds of books on a single device without the need for physical storage space. In addition, e-books offer useful features such as adjustable text size and built-in dictionaries, which many readers find helpful, particularly for study or travel purposes.

On the other hand, many people argue that paper books will never fully disappear, since they offer a reading experience that digital formats cannot completely replicate. Many readers enjoy the physical sensation of holding a book, turning pages, and reading without the distraction of notifications or screen glare. Paper books are also often preferred for gifting, collecting, or reading before bed, since they avoid the eye strain associated with prolonged screen use. For example, many book lovers continue to purchase physical copies of their favourite novels, even when e-book versions are available and more affordable.

In my opinion, both formats will likely continue to coexist rather than one completely replacing the other, since they appeal to different reading preferences and situations.

In conclusion, while e-books offer clear convenience, paper books provide a unique reading experience that many people continue to value, meaning both formats are likely to remain popular for different reasons.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Libraries should focus on improving their technological resources rather than on building a larger collection of paper books. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that libraries should prioritise technology over expanding their paper book collections. I partly agree with this view, as technology offers significant benefits, although paper books still play an important role within libraries.

One reason I support increasing technological resources is that digital tools greatly expand what libraries can offer their communities. Providing computers, internet access, and digital databases allows visitors to access a much wider range of information than physical books alone could ever provide, particularly for research or academic study. In addition, digital resources take up far less physical space than paper books, allowing libraries to offer a wider variety of services, such as study areas or community programmes, within the same building. For example, many libraries have successfully expanded their services by offering free computer access and digital resources for visitors who cannot afford these tools at home.

However, I do not believe paper books should be neglected entirely. Many people, particularly older visitors or young children, still prefer reading physical books, and libraries play an important role in providing access to reading materials for those without the means to buy books or digital devices themselves. Removing paper books entirely could disadvantage certain groups within the community who rely on libraries specifically for printed reading materials.

In conclusion, while I agree that libraries should invest more in technological resources to meet changing needs, I do not believe this should come at the expense of maintaining a reasonable paper book collection, since both formats continue to serve different visitors effectively.

Advantages & Disadvantages

Public libraries will soon no longer be housed in a building as all facilities and books will be available online for everyone to access. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of public libraries only existing online.

Some people predict that public libraries will eventually move entirely online, without any physical buildings. This shift would bring certain advantages, but it would also create some significant disadvantages for library users.

One clear advantage of fully online libraries is increased accessibility. People living in remote areas, or those unable to travel due to disability or lack of transport, would be able to access books and resources from home at any time, without being limited by a library's opening hours. Online libraries would also reduce costs associated with maintaining physical buildings, potentially allowing more funding to be directed towards expanding digital collections and resources instead.

However, moving libraries entirely online would also create serious disadvantages. Physical libraries currently serve as important community spaces, offering quiet areas for studying, meeting rooms, and social activities that an online-only service could not replace. This would particularly disadvantage people who do not have reliable internet access or suitable devices at home, including many elderly people and low-income families, potentially increasing existing inequalities in access to information. Additionally, librarians provide valuable in-person assistance and guidance, particularly for research or learning new skills, which can be more difficult to replicate through an entirely online platform.

In conclusion, while an entirely online library system would improve accessibility and reduce costs, it would also remove valuable community spaces and disadvantage those without reliable internet access. A combination of physical and online library services would likely serve communities more effectively than removing physical libraries altogether.
