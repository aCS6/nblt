Some people think that dangerous sports should be banned, while others think people should be free to choose. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

There is disagreement about whether dangerous sports should be banned for safety reasons, or whether individuals should have the freedom to choose which sports they participate in. This essay will discuss both views before giving my opinion.

Those who support banning dangerous sports argue that activities such as extreme skiing, base jumping, or boxing carry serious risks of injury or even death. Governments and sports organisations, they argue, have a responsibility to protect citizens from unnecessary harm, particularly since accidents can also place a financial burden on healthcare systems. Banning particularly hazardous sports could prevent needless injuries and save lives, especially among younger or inexperienced participants.

On the other hand, many people believe individuals should have the right to make their own choices, including participating in risky activities, as long as they are only endangering themselves. Supporters of this view argue that many everyday activities carry some level of risk, and banning dangerous sports would be an unreasonable restriction on personal freedom. Additionally, dangerous sports often provide participants with valuable experiences, such as excitement, personal achievement, and improved physical fitness, which they may not find elsewhere. With proper safety training and equipment, many of these risks can also be significantly reduced.

In my opinion, rather than imposing outright bans, governments should focus on ensuring dangerous sports are properly regulated, with strict safety standards and mandatory training for participants. This approach protects individual freedom while still reducing unnecessary risks.

In conclusion, while dangerous sports do carry genuine risks, I believe personal freedom should generally be respected, provided appropriate safety regulations are in place to minimise unnecessary harm.

Some people think that sport teaches children how to compete, while others believe that children learn teamwork. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

There is debate about whether the main value of sport for children lies in teaching them how to compete, or in teaching them how to work as part of a team. This essay will discuss both views before giving my opinion.

Those who believe sport primarily teaches competition argue that children learn valuable life skills through the experience of winning and losing. Competitive sport helps children develop resilience, determination, and the motivation to improve their performance, qualities that can benefit them throughout their lives, including in future careers. Learning how to handle both victory and defeat gracefully is often seen as an important part of personal development that competitive sport specifically provides.

On the other hand, many people argue that team sports primarily teach children the value of cooperation and working effectively with others. In sports such as football or basketball, children must learn to communicate, share responsibility, and support their teammates in order to succeed collectively, rather than focusing solely on individual achievement. These teamwork skills are often just as valuable in adult life, particularly in workplace environments that require effective collaboration.

In my opinion, sport actually teaches children both skills simultaneously, rather than one being more important than the other. Most team sports naturally combine competitive elements with the need for cooperation, helping children develop a balanced set of important life skills.

In conclusion, while sport can teach children valuable lessons about both competition and teamwork, I believe these two skills are closely connected and are usually developed together through participation in team sports.

Some people think that introducing children to team sports is the best way to teach children teamwork. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that team sports are the most effective way to teach children how to work well with others. I largely agree with this view, although I believe other activities can also help develop teamwork skills.

One reason I support this opinion is that team sports naturally require children to cooperate in order to achieve a shared goal. Whether playing football, basketball, or volleyball, children must learn to communicate with teammates, share responsibilities, and support one another during both success and failure. These experiences help children understand that individual performance alone is not enough to succeed; the whole team must work well together. For example, a child who learns to pass the ball to a better-positioned teammate, rather than trying to score alone, begins to understand the value of collective effort over individual achievement.

However, team sports are not the only way children can learn teamwork. Group projects at school, drama productions, or musical ensembles also require children to collaborate closely with others towards a shared outcome. These activities can teach similar skills, such as communication and compromise, without necessarily involving competitive sport.

In conclusion, I largely agree that team sports are an excellent way to teach children teamwork, given the natural cooperation required to succeed. However, other collaborative activities can also help children develop these important skills, meaning sport should not be considered the only effective method.

Some people believe that physical exercise should be the responsibility of parents, meaning schools should not include sports lessons within their curriculum. I disagree with this view, as I believe schools play an essential role in ensuring children receive sufficient exercise.

While parents undoubtedly play an important role in encouraging healthy habits at home, not all families have the time, resources, or knowledge needed to ensure their children exercise regularly. Some parents work long hours or lack access to suitable facilities, meaning children may not receive adequate physical activity outside school without structured support. Schools provide a consistent, equal opportunity for all children to exercise, regardless of their family's individual circumstances.

In addition, physical education lessons offer benefits beyond simple exercise. School sports help children develop teamwork, discipline, and social skills, while also introducing them to a variety of sports and physical activities they might not otherwise experience at home. Regular exercise during school hours can also improve concentration and academic performance, meaning sports lessons may actually support, rather than take away from, children's overall education.

In conclusion, I disagree that schools should remove sports lessons from the curriculum. Physical education plays a valuable role in ensuring all children receive sufficient exercise, regardless of their family circumstances, while also offering additional social and educational benefits.

Many countries want to host international sports events, while other countries think that hosting sports events has more problems than benefits. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Many countries compete for the opportunity to host major international sports events, while others believe hosting such events creates more problems than benefits. This essay will discuss both views before presenting my opinion.

Those who support hosting international sports events argue that these events can bring substantial economic benefits, including increased tourism, job creation, and improved infrastructure, such as new stadiums, transport systems, and hotels. Hosting a major event can also boost national pride and international reputation, showcasing a country's culture and organisational capabilities to a global audience. For example, cities that have hosted the Olympic Games often see long-term benefits from improved public transport and sports facilities built specifically for the event.

On the other hand, many people argue that hosting large sports events often creates more problems than benefits. The enormous costs involved in building new stadiums and infrastructure can leave host countries with significant debt, particularly if facilities are not used effectively after the event concludes. Additionally, local residents can be negatively affected by disruption during construction, increased living costs, and, in some cases, forced relocation to make way for new facilities.

In my opinion, whether hosting a sports event proves beneficial largely depends on careful planning and long-term infrastructure use. Countries that plan realistically, ensuring facilities remain useful after the event, are more likely to benefit than those focused solely on short-term prestige.

In conclusion, while hosting sports events can offer significant economic and cultural benefits, poor planning can lead to serious financial and social problems, meaning careful preparation is essential for success.

Some people think that companies should provide employees with exercise time during the day. What is your opinion about this?

Some people believe that companies should allow employees dedicated time during the working day for exercise. I agree with this view, as I believe this practice would benefit both employees and employers.

One reason I support this opinion is that regular exercise significantly improves physical and mental health, which can directly benefit workplace performance. Employees who have the opportunity to exercise during the day often experience reduced stress levels and improved concentration, making them more productive during their remaining working hours. For example, companies that have introduced short exercise breaks or on-site gym facilities have often reported improvements in employee morale and reduced sick leave.

In addition, providing exercise time demonstrates that companies genuinely care about their employees' wellbeing, which can improve job satisfaction and help retain talented staff. Many employees today value workplace benefits that support a healthy work-life balance, and offering exercise time can make a company more attractive to potential employees in a competitive job market.

However, companies would need to implement this policy carefully to avoid negatively affecting productivity or creating unfairness between employees with different job roles. Clear guidelines, such as limiting exercise breaks to a reasonable amount of time, would help ensure this benefit does not disrupt normal business operations.

In conclusion, I agree that companies should provide employees with time to exercise during the working day, given the clear benefits to health, productivity, and job satisfaction, provided this is implemented in a fair and structured way.

With an increasing overweight population some people think universities should make sport a compulsory module on all degree courses. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that, given rising obesity rates, universities should make sport a compulsory part of every degree course. I disagree with this view, as I believe this approach would be impractical and unnecessary, despite understanding the health concerns behind it.

It is true that regular exercise offers significant health benefits, and encouraging physical activity among university students could help address rising obesity levels. Universities could play a positive role in promoting healthier lifestyles among young adults, who often develop lasting habits during this stage of their lives.

However, making sport compulsory for all degree courses would create considerable practical problems. Students already have demanding academic schedules, and adding mandatory sports classes could take valuable time away from their main subjects, particularly for students studying intensive courses such as medicine or engineering. Additionally, students already have differing levels of interest and physical ability, meaning a compulsory approach could feel unfair or unenjoyable for many, potentially discouraging rather than encouraging a positive attitude towards exercise. A more effective solution would be for universities to offer optional sports facilities, clubs, and affordable gym access, encouraging students to exercise voluntarily rather than forcing participation.

In conclusion, while promoting exercise among university students is important, I disagree that sport should be made a compulsory module on all degree courses. Providing accessible, optional opportunities for exercise would likely be a more effective and practical solution.

Professional sports people are often idolised by young children. Some people think that they, therefore, have a responsibility to be good role models at all times for children. Do you agree or disagree?

Some people believe that because children often look up to professional athletes, these athletes have a responsibility to behave as good role models at all times. I largely agree with this view, given the significant influence athletes can have on young fans.

One reason I support this opinion is that children frequently imitate the behaviour of athletes they admire, both on and off the field. When professional athletes display good sportsmanship, discipline, and respect for opponents, this can positively influence how children behave in their own sporting activities and everyday lives. Conversely, athletes who behave poorly, whether through unsportsmanlike conduct or personal scandals, can set a damaging example for impressionable young fans who may see such behaviour as acceptable or admirable, simply because it comes from someone they respect.

However, I also believe it is important to remember that athletes are human and cannot be expected to behave perfectly at all times. Placing excessive pressure on athletes to maintain a flawless public image may be unrealistic and could create unnecessary stress in their personal lives. Parents and educators should also play an active role in guiding children's understanding of appropriate behaviour, rather than relying solely on athletes to set a good example.

In conclusion, while I agree that professional athletes carry a responsibility to act as positive role models given their influence on children, this expectation should be reasonable, recognising that athletes, like anyone else, are not perfect.

Some people think that sports involving violence, such as boxing and martial arts, should be banned from TV as well as from international sporting competitions. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that violent sports, such as boxing and martial arts, should be banned from television broadcasts and international competitions. I disagree with this view, as I believe these sports have cultural and personal value that justifies their continued presence, provided proper safety measures are maintained.

It is true that combat sports carry inherent risks of injury, and broadcasting violent contact could potentially normalise aggressive behaviour, particularly among younger viewers. Critics argue that removing these sports from television and international competitions could help reduce exposure to violence and protect athletes from serious harm.

However, sports like boxing and martial arts have deep historical and cultural significance in many countries, representing discipline, skill, and tradition rather than simple violence. Participants in these sports typically undergo extensive training and follow strict safety regulations, including protective equipment and medical supervision, which significantly reduce the risk of serious injury compared to how these sports may sometimes be perceived. Additionally, banning these sports from television would deny athletes the recognition and financial opportunities that come from professional competition, while also removing a legitimate form of entertainment that many people genuinely enjoy watching.

In conclusion, while safety concerns surrounding violent sports are valid, I disagree that they should be banned from television or international competitions. With proper regulation and safety standards already in place, these sports can continue to be enjoyed responsibly by both participants and audiences.
