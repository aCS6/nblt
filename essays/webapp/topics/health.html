Health

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

The prevention of health problems and illness is more important than treatment and medicine. Government funding should reflect this. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that preventing illness is more important than treating it, and that governments should direct more funding towards prevention rather than medicine. I largely agree with this view, as prevention is often more effective and less costly in the long run.

One reason I support this opinion is that preventing illness reduces the overall burden on healthcare systems. When governments invest in campaigns that encourage healthy eating, regular exercise, and routine health screenings, fewer people develop serious conditions such as diabetes or heart disease in the first place. This ultimately saves money, since treating chronic illnesses over many years is far more expensive than preventing them early. For example, vaccination programmes have successfully reduced the spread of diseases like measles, saving governments significant amounts that would otherwise be spent on treatment.

However, I do not believe treatment and medicine should be neglected entirely. Some illnesses, such as accidents or genetic conditions, cannot be prevented no matter how much money is invested in public health campaigns. People suffering from these conditions still need access to effective medical treatment, and reducing funding in this area could put lives at risk. Therefore, a balanced approach, with a stronger focus on prevention, is more sensible than cutting treatment funding altogether.

In conclusion, I agree that prevention deserves greater government investment because it reduces long-term costs and improves public health overall. Nevertheless, treatment services must still be adequately funded to care for those who become ill despite these preventive efforts.

Problem & Solution

The number of people who are at risk of serious health problems due to being overweight is increasing. What is the reason for the growth in overweight people in society? How can this problem be solved?

In recent years, the number of overweight people facing serious health risks has grown considerably. This essay will examine the main causes of this trend and suggest some practical solutions.

One of the primary reasons for rising obesity rates is the widespread availability of unhealthy, calorie-dense food. Fast food restaurants and processed snacks are now easy to access and often cheaper than healthier alternatives, encouraging people to make poor dietary choices. In addition, modern lifestyles have become increasingly sedentary. Many jobs now involve sitting at a desk for long hours, while leisure time is often spent watching television or using smartphones rather than engaging in physical activity. This combination of poor diet and inactivity has contributed significantly to weight gain across many societies.

To address this problem, governments could introduce taxes on sugary and high-fat foods, making healthier options more financially attractive by comparison. Public health campaigns could also raise awareness about the risks of being overweight and provide practical advice on nutrition and exercise. Furthermore, employers could be encouraged to promote physical activity in the workplace, for example by providing gym discounts or organising walking meetings. Schools also have an important role to play by ensuring children learn healthy habits early, through both physical education and nutrition classes.

In conclusion, rising obesity rates are largely caused by poor diet and inactive lifestyles. However, through a combination of taxation, public education, and workplace initiatives, governments and communities can work together to reverse this worrying trend.

Two-Part Question

The amount of time spent on sport and exercise should be increased in schools in order to tackle the problem of overweight children. Do you think this is the best way to deal with the problem? What other solutions can you suggest?

Childhood obesity is a growing concern, and some people believe that increasing the amount of physical education in schools is the best way to address it. While I agree this would help, I do not believe it is the only solution needed.

Increasing sport and exercise time in schools would certainly benefit children's health. Regular physical activity helps burn calories, strengthens the body, and can instil healthy habits that children carry into adulthood. Schools already provide a structured environment where all children, regardless of their family background, can access exercise equally. For example, schools that have introduced daily physical activity sessions have often reported improvements in students' fitness levels and general wellbeing. However, exercise alone cannot fully solve the problem, since diet plays an equally important role in weight management, and this issue often begins at home rather than at school.

Other solutions should therefore be considered alongside increased school sport. Improving the quality of food served in school canteens would help ensure children eat healthily during the school day. Parents also need support and education on preparing balanced meals, since eating habits established at home have a lasting impact on children's weight. In addition, limiting the advertising of unhealthy food aimed at children could reduce the pressure they feel to consume sugary snacks and fast food.

In conclusion, while increasing exercise in schools is a valuable step towards tackling childhood obesity, it should be combined with better school meals, parental education, and stricter advertising regulations to achieve the best results.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

More and more people are suffering from health problems caused by a modern lifestyle which cannot be treated with modern medicines. Some people think that a return to traditional medicine should be encouraged. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that as modern lifestyles cause more health problems that conventional medicine struggles to treat, society should return to traditional medicine. I only partly agree with this view, as I believe traditional and modern medicine can work together rather than one replacing the other.

It is true that many health problems today, such as chronic stress, anxiety, and lifestyle-related illnesses, are not always effectively treated by modern medicine alone. Traditional practices, such as herbal remedies, acupuncture, or meditation, have been used for centuries and can offer genuine benefits, particularly for managing stress and improving general wellbeing. For example, many people find that practices like yoga or traditional herbal treatments help reduce symptoms of anxiety in ways that complement conventional treatment.

However, I do not believe traditional medicine should replace modern medicine entirely. Modern medical advances, such as antibiotics, surgery, and vaccines, have saved countless lives and successfully treat many conditions that traditional methods alone cannot cure. Relying solely on traditional remedies for serious illnesses, such as infections or heart disease, could be extremely dangerous. Instead, a combined approach, where traditional practices support mental and physical wellbeing while modern medicine treats specific illnesses, would likely produce the best outcomes for patients.

In conclusion, while traditional medicine can offer valuable benefits, particularly for stress-related conditions, I disagree that it should fully replace modern medicine. A combination of both approaches is more likely to address the wide range of health problems people face today.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Health care should not be provided for free regardless of a person's income. The health of a person is in their own hands and they should, therefore, be held accountable for that. Do you agree with this opinion?

Some people believe that healthcare should not be free for everyone, arguing that individuals are responsible for their own health and should bear the cost accordingly. I disagree with this opinion, as I believe free healthcare is essential for a fair and functioning society.

It is true that lifestyle choices, such as diet, exercise, and smoking, influence a person's health significantly. Someone who eats poorly or avoids exercise may be more likely to develop health problems later in life. From this perspective, it may seem reasonable to argue that individuals should pay for the consequences of their own choices rather than relying on public funding.

However, health is not always within a person's control. Genetic conditions, accidents, and diseases can affect anyone, regardless of how carefully they live. If healthcare were not provided for free, many people, particularly those with lower incomes, would be unable to afford essential treatment, leading to unnecessary suffering and even preventable deaths. Furthermore, denying healthcare based on income would deepen existing inequalities, as wealthier individuals could access treatment while poorer citizens could not. Countries with strong public healthcare systems, such as the United Kingdom's National Health Service, demonstrate that free healthcare can improve overall public health and reduce inequality, even though such systems face funding challenges.

In conclusion, I disagree that healthcare should only be available to those who can pay. While personal responsibility for health is important, free access to healthcare remains essential for fairness and for protecting vulnerable members of society.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

The best way to improve health is to exercise daily. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that daily exercise is the best way to improve health. I largely agree with this statement, although I believe other factors, such as diet and sleep, are equally important for achieving good overall health.

Regular exercise offers numerous physical and mental health benefits. Physically, it strengthens the heart, improves circulation, and helps maintain a healthy body weight, reducing the risk of conditions such as heart disease and diabetes. Exercise also has significant mental health benefits, as physical activity releases hormones that reduce stress and improve mood. For example, many people who exercise regularly, such as through daily walking or jogging, report feeling more energetic and experiencing fewer symptoms of anxiety or depression.

However, exercise alone cannot guarantee good health. A person who exercises daily but eats an unhealthy diet high in processed food may still develop health problems, such as high cholesterol or nutrient deficiencies. Similarly, insufficient sleep can weaken the immune system and negatively affect both physical and mental health, regardless of how much a person exercises. Therefore, while daily exercise is extremely beneficial, it should be combined with a balanced diet and adequate rest to achieve the best possible health outcomes.

In conclusion, I agree that daily exercise plays a major role in improving health, particularly by strengthening the body and reducing stress. However, true overall health also depends on maintaining a healthy diet and getting sufficient sleep alongside regular physical activity.

Discussion

Some people think that public health is the responsibility of the government while others think that people should be responsible for their own health. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.

There is ongoing debate about whether public health should be the government's responsibility or whether individuals should take responsibility for their own health. While both views have merit, I believe responsibility should be shared between the two.

Those who argue that public health is the government's responsibility point out that governments control many factors that influence health on a national scale. Governments regulate food safety, fund public healthcare systems, and can introduce policies such as taxes on unhealthy products or restrictions on tobacco advertising. Without such regulation, harmful products could be marketed freely, and vulnerable groups, such as children, might be exposed to serious health risks. Public health campaigns run by governments have also successfully reduced smoking rates in many countries.

On the other hand, many people believe that individuals must take responsibility for their own health choices. No government policy can force someone to eat healthily, exercise regularly, or avoid harmful habits such as smoking or excessive drinking. Ultimately, daily lifestyle decisions rest with the individual, and personal accountability plays a crucial role in preventing many common health problems.

In conclusion, I believe both the government and individuals share responsibility for public health. Governments should create policies and environments that support healthy living, such as regulating harmful products and funding healthcare, while individuals must take ownership of their personal daily choices to maintain good health.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Doctors should be responsible for educating their patients about how to improve their health. Do you agree with this?

Some people believe that doctors should take responsibility for teaching patients how to improve their health. I agree with this view, as doctors are well positioned to provide accurate, personalised health advice that can prevent future illness.

One reason I support this opinion is that doctors have the medical expertise needed to identify specific health risks for each patient. During routine check-ups, doctors can advise patients on issues such as diet, exercise, or smoking based on the patient's individual health history and current condition. This personalised guidance is often more effective than general public health campaigns, since it addresses each person's specific circumstances. For example, a doctor who notices early signs of high blood pressure can give tailored advice on diet and lifestyle changes that could prevent the condition from worsening.

Furthermore, patients are often more likely to trust and follow advice given directly by a medical professional than information from other sources, such as social media or advertisements. Doctors are seen as credible and knowledgeable, which increases the likelihood that patients will take their recommendations seriously. This trust can lead to real improvements in patients' long-term health outcomes.

However, doctors already have limited time with each patient, so schools, media, and public health organisations should also share responsibility for educating the public. Relying on doctors alone may not reach people who rarely visit a doctor in the first place.

In conclusion, I agree that doctors should play a key role in educating patients about their health, given their expertise and the trust patients place in them. Nevertheless, this responsibility should be shared with other institutions to ensure wider public health education.

Problem & Solution

Walking is known to be beneficial for health and yet fewer and fewer people are walking these days. What are the reasons for this? What can be done to tackle this problem?

Although walking is widely recognised as beneficial for health, fewer people choose to walk nowadays. This essay will explore the reasons behind this trend and suggest possible solutions.

One major reason for the decline in walking is the increasing reliance on cars and public transport for even short journeys. Many people find driving more convenient and time-saving, especially in cities where distances between home, work, and shops can be inconvenient to walk. In addition, busy modern lifestyles leave many people feeling they do not have enough time to walk when faster transport options are available. Poor urban planning also plays a role, as some cities lack safe, pleasant pathways or green spaces that encourage walking, making it a less appealing option compared to driving.

To address this issue, city planners could invest in improving pedestrian infrastructure, such as building wider, safer pavements and creating more parks and walking trails. Encouraging shorter car journeys to be replaced with walking, for example through public awareness campaigns highlighting the health benefits, could also help change public attitudes. Employers could contribute by promoting walking during breaks or encouraging employees to walk or cycle to work rather than drive, perhaps by providing changing facilities or secure bicycle storage.

In conclusion, the decline in walking is largely due to convenient transport options and poor urban design. However, through better infrastructure, public awareness campaigns, and workplace encouragement, more people could be encouraged to incorporate walking into their daily lives.
