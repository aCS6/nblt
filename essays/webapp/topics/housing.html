Housing

Advantages & Disadvantages

In some countries, most people prefer to rent their homes rather than buying them. What are the advantages and disadvantages of renting a home?

In many countries, renting a home has become more common than buying one. This trend brings certain benefits, but it also comes with some drawbacks that renters need to consider.

One major advantage of renting is flexibility. People who rent can move more easily for work, study, or personal reasons, without the long process of selling a property. This is particularly useful for young professionals who may need to relocate frequently for job opportunities. Renting also avoids the large upfront costs associated with buying, such as a deposit or mortgage fees, which makes it more accessible for people who do not have significant savings. For example, someone starting a new career can rent an apartment close to work without needing years of savings first.

However, renting also has notable disadvantages. Renters do not build any equity in the property, meaning that the money spent on rent does not contribute to owning an asset in the long run. Unlike homeowners, renters can also face uncertainty, as landlords may decide to raise rent or ask tenants to leave when a contract ends. This lack of stability can make it harder for families to settle permanently in one place, especially when it comes to keeping children in the same school. Additionally, renters often have limited freedom to renovate or personalise their living space.

In conclusion, renting offers flexibility and lower upfront costs, making it attractive for people who value mobility. However, the lack of long-term financial benefit and housing security are significant drawbacks that many renters eventually have to weigh against the advantages.

Planting trees is very important for the environment. Some people say trees should be planted in the vacant areas of cities and towns, while others say housing facilities should be built instead. Do you agree or disagree?

Some people believe that empty spaces in cities should be used to plant trees, while others think this land would be better used for building homes. I agree that planting trees should be the priority, as the environmental benefits outweigh the need for additional housing in most cases.

One reason I support planting trees is that urban areas urgently need more green spaces to counter the effects of pollution and rising temperatures. Trees absorb carbon dioxide, produce oxygen, and help cool cities during hot weather, which has become an increasingly serious issue as urban areas continue to grow. In addition, green spaces improve residents' mental wellbeing by providing places to relax and exercise. For example, cities that have invested in urban parks and tree-planting projects, such as Singapore, have seen improvements in both air quality and public satisfaction with city life.

Of course, the need for housing cannot be ignored, particularly in cities facing serious shortages and rising property prices. Building homes on vacant land can help address overcrowding and make housing more affordable. However, I believe this housing shortage should be solved primarily by developing existing buildings upward or renovating unused structures, rather than removing potential green spaces, which are much harder to recreate once lost.

In conclusion, while housing needs are important, I believe planting trees in vacant urban areas should be prioritised because of the significant environmental and health benefits they provide. Housing shortages can often be addressed through other means, such as better use of existing buildings.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Cities should be designed to be attractive for local people and foreign tourists. Do you agree or disagree?

Some people believe that city planning should focus on making cities appealing to both residents and tourists. I agree with this view, as I believe attractive cities benefit both groups without creating a real conflict between their needs.

One reason I support this idea is that many features that make a city attractive to tourists also improve quality of life for residents. Well-maintained parks, clean streets, and accessible public transport are appreciated by both groups equally. For example, cities like Copenhagen have invested heavily in cycling infrastructure and green spaces, which attract tourists while also making daily life more pleasant and healthier for local residents.

In addition, tourism brings significant economic benefits that can be reinvested into the city for residents' advantage. Money generated from tourism can fund public services, such as improved public transport or cultural facilities, which locals also use regularly. Historical landmarks and cultural sites, often preserved to attract visitors, also give residents a stronger sense of local identity and pride in their city's heritage.

However, city planners must be careful to prevent tourism from overwhelming local life, such as excessive short-term rental properties driving up housing costs for residents. Careful planning is needed to balance tourist attractions with affordable housing and functional infrastructure for local people.

In conclusion, I agree that cities should be designed with both residents and tourists in mind, since well-planned attractive spaces generally benefit both groups. Nevertheless, careful management is necessary to ensure tourism does not come at the expense of residents' daily needs.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Services, such as shops, schools and commercial centres should be relocated out of the city centre. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that essential services like shops, schools, and commercial centres should be moved away from city centres. I disagree with this view, as I believe keeping these services centrally located benefits most residents.

One reason I oppose relocating these services is that city centres are usually the most accessible areas, particularly for people who rely on public transport rather than private cars. Moving shops and schools to the outskirts would make daily life more difficult for residents without vehicles, including elderly people, students, and low-income families. For example, if schools were relocated far from residential areas, many children would face longer, more inconvenient commutes, which could affect their education and wellbeing.

In addition, keeping commercial centres in the city centre helps maintain a lively, functional urban environment. Areas with a mix of shops, offices, and services tend to have better infrastructure and remain active throughout the day, which can reduce crime and support local businesses. Relocating services outward could leave city centres empty and underused, leading to economic decline in those areas.

However, some services, such as large shopping malls or industrial commercial centres, could reasonably be located on the outskirts, since these often require large amounts of space that is scarce in city centres. This would not create the same accessibility problems as relocating essential services like schools.

In conclusion, I disagree that shops, schools, and commercial centres should generally be moved out of city centres, as this would reduce accessibility for many residents. Only larger, space-intensive services should be considered for relocation to the outskirts.

Discussion

Some people think that old buildings should be protected, while others believe that they should be replaced by newer buildings. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.

There is disagreement about whether old buildings should be preserved or replaced with modern structures. While both views have valid points, I believe old buildings should generally be protected, except in cases where they are no longer structurally safe or practical.

Those who support protecting old buildings argue that they represent a country's history and cultural identity. Historic buildings often attract tourists, generate income for local businesses, and give residents a sense of connection to their city's past. For example, cities like Rome and Paris have preserved many historic sites, which continue to attract millions of visitors each year and contribute significantly to their economies.

On the other hand, those who favour replacing old buildings argue that many older structures are inefficient, difficult to maintain, and unsuitable for modern needs. New buildings can be designed with better insulation, more efficient use of space, and modern safety standards, including earthquake resistance in some regions. Additionally, as cities grow and populations increase, more modern housing or office space may be urgently needed, and rebuilding can sometimes be more practical than renovating older structures.

In conclusion, I believe old buildings should generally be preserved, particularly those with historical or cultural significance, because they cannot be recreated once demolished. However, buildings that are unsafe or beyond practical repair should be replaced, provided this is done without unnecessarily destroying a city's architectural heritage.

Opinion (Agree/Disagree)

Many countries are experiencing population growth and need more homes. Should these new homes be constructed in existing cities or should new towns be built in the countryside?

As populations continue to grow in many countries, governments must decide whether to build additional housing within existing cities or create entirely new towns in rural areas. In my opinion, expanding existing cities is generally the better solution, although new towns can play a supporting role.

One key advantage of building homes within existing cities is that essential infrastructure, such as roads, public transport, hospitals, and schools, is already in place. Expanding housing in these areas, for example by constructing taller apartment buildings, allows new residents to access these services immediately without requiring significant new investment. This approach also helps prevent urban sprawl from consuming valuable agricultural or natural land surrounding cities.

However, building entirely new towns in the countryside also has benefits worth considering. New towns can be carefully planned from the beginning, allowing for better traffic management, more green spaces, and modern infrastructure designed to meet current needs, rather than being limited by older city layouts. For example, several countries have successfully built new towns that relieve pressure on overcrowded capital cities while offering residents a higher quality of life through well-planned neighbourhoods.

In conclusion, while new towns offer benefits such as modern planning and reduced city overcrowding, I believe expanding existing cities remains the more practical solution overall, since it makes better use of infrastructure that already exists. New towns should be considered a secondary option where cities have reached their practical limits.

Two-Part Question

Nowadays, a lot of buildings, such as offices and schools, are being built with an open plan design. Why is that? Do you see this as a positive or negative development?

In recent years, open plan design has become increasingly popular for buildings such as offices and schools. This essay will explore the reasons behind this trend and evaluate whether it represents a positive or negative development.

One major reason for this shift is cost efficiency. Open plan spaces require fewer walls and internal structures, which reduces construction costs and allows for more flexible use of space. Businesses in particular favour open plan offices because they can easily be reorganised as the company grows or changes, without the expense of rebuilding walls. Additionally, open plan designs are believed to encourage better communication and collaboration among employees or students, since physical barriers between individuals are reduced.

In my opinion, this development brings both benefits and drawbacks, making it a mixed development overall. On the positive side, open spaces can create a more collaborative and flexible environment, allowing teams to work together more easily and adapt spaces for different activities. However, open plan buildings also have significant downsides. Noise levels tend to be much higher, making it difficult for people to concentrate, particularly in schools where students need quiet environments for learning. Privacy is also reduced, which can be uncomfortable for employees discussing sensitive matters or students who prefer a quieter setting for individual study.

In conclusion, while open plan design offers cost savings and encourages collaboration, I believe it is not entirely positive, given the problems it creates with noise and privacy. A more effective approach might combine open areas with some private spaces to balance these benefits and drawbacks.

Cities should prioritise parks rather than housing developments. To what extent do you agree?

Some people believe that cities should focus on creating parks rather than building more housing. I only partly agree with this view, as I believe both parks and housing are essential, and cities must balance the two rather than prioritising one over the other.

One reason parks deserve significant attention is their contribution to public health and environmental quality. Parks provide spaces for exercise, relaxation, and social interaction, which improve residents' physical and mental wellbeing. They also help reduce air pollution and urban heat, particularly important as cities become more crowded and built-up. For example, cities with well-maintained parks, such as New York's Central Park, demonstrate how green spaces can improve quality of life even within a densely populated city.

However, housing remains an urgent priority in many cities, particularly where population growth has led to serious shortages and rapidly rising property prices. Without sufficient housing, many residents face overcrowding or unaffordable rent, which can cause significant social problems. Prioritising parks over housing in such circumstances could worsen an already difficult situation for many families.

In conclusion, while parks provide valuable health and environmental benefits, I do not believe cities should prioritise them over housing developments. Instead, city planners should aim to integrate both, for example by including small green spaces within new housing developments, ensuring residents benefit from both adequate housing and accessible parks.
