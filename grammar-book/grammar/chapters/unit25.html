B Grammar

Nominalisation in Written English

When we choose to give the main information in a clause as a noun phrase rather than as a verb, this is called nominalisation.

1 Nominalisation in written English

Spoken English
  • ◆ Actions/events as verbs: swell up
  • ◆ Events happen to people: I reacted badly, my foot began to go red
  • ◆ Personal pronouns as subjects: I, she
  • ◆ Action/event verbs: reacted, trod, itched
Written English
  • ◆ Actions/events as nouns: swelling
  • ◆ Events expressed impersonally: the normal reaction is…
  • ◆ Nouns as subjects: a person, the throat
  • ◆ Non-action verbs: be, have
  • ◆ Sentences have a lot of vocabulary words

We can also change adjectives to nouns:

The cathedral is tall and can be seen from all over the town.

The cathedral's height makes it visible all over town.

2 Reasons for using nominalisation

Making texts impersonal and authoritative

By turning actions into nouns we make the text less personal and more authoritative. We avoid personal pronouns.

Adding information

Nominalisation is particularly useful for Academic Writing Task 1 because we can count, describe and classify noun phrases in ways we cannot do with verbs:

count: the two charts

describe: the two coloured charts

classify: the two coloured bar charts

Avoiding repetition

We can use nominalisation to refer back to a previously mentioned idea:

The number of unemployed increased by 5% last month. The reason for this increase is still unclear.

Nominalisation also helps paraphrase in IELTS Listening and Reading:

Listening text: Then I began to find it difficult to breathe and kept coughing.

Question options: A breathing problems   C a cough → both are nominalisations of the verbs used.

D Test Practice

Academic Writing Task 1

Spend time studying the information first. Try to identify key areas in the graph where the data changes considerably or where there is no change over a period of time. Do not miss out any important details and make sure you stick to the time limit.

WRITING TASK 1

You should spend 20 minutes on this task.

The graph below shows the population figures of different types of wild birds in the United Kingdom between 1970 and 2004.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.

Graph data (Population of wild birds 1970–2004, Index 1970=100):
• All species (111): relatively stable overall, notable drops 1977–79 and 1982–86, improvement since 1994, slight decrease 2000
• Coastal species (20): steady increase to ~135 by 2004
• Woodland species (33): decline ~30% between 1974–1992, slight increase 2000, dropped again 2004
• Farmland species (19): severe decline from 1978, fell dramatically, rate of decline slowed since 2000, remains ~60

Model answer

The total number of all species of wild birds in the UK has been relatively stable over the last two decades, although there were significant drops in numbers between 1977 and 1979 and again in 1982 and 1986. Since 1994 there has been an improvement in numbers of all species, although the graph shows a slight decrease in 2000.

In contrast, the population of farmland birds has suffered a severe decline, particularly in 1978 when numbers fell dramatically. The rate of decline in the population appears to have slowed and it has remained relatively stable since 2000.

There was also a fall in the number of woodland birds of around 30% between 1974 and 1992. The population increased slightly in 2000 but it had dropped again by 2004.

It is only coastal species that have seen a steady increase in population over this period. Their numbers dropped slightly in 1995 and again in 2003 but the overall trend has been a positive one.
