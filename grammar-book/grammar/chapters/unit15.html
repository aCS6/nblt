B Grammar

We can use reported speech to report in speech or in writing what someone has said. When we report what someone has said, we sometimes change the tense of the main verb: we move the tenses 'back' one tense.

B1 — Tense changes

Original tense of main verbTense in reported speech
present simple — I live in Italy.past simple — She said she lived in Italy.
present continuous — I'm living in Italy.past continuous — She said she was living in Italy.
past simple — I lived in Italy.past perfect — She said she'd / had lived in Italy.
past continuous — I was living in Italy.past perfect continuous — She said she had been living in Italy.
present perfect — I've lived in Italy.past perfect — She said she had lived in Italy.
past perfect — I'd lived in Italy.past perfect (no change) — She said she'd lived in Italy.
can — I can live in Italy.could — She said she could live in Italy.
will — I'll live in Italy.would — She said she would live in Italy.
must — I must live in Italy.had to — She said she had to live in Italy.
may / might — I may/might live in Italy.might — She said she might live in Italy.
be going to — I'm going to live in Italy.was/were going to — She said she was going to live in Italy.
  • If the original tense was past simple or past continuous, we can keep the past form unchanged: 'We decided to change the way we operate our business two years ago.' → West claimed that they decided to make changes two years ago.
  • We often choose not to change the tense. This may be because what we are talking about remains true: 'This has nothing to do with the figures.' → He said that the job losses have nothing to do with the figures. (at the time of reporting, this fact is still true)

B2 — Reporting verbs

  • reporting verb (+ that) — agree, admit, announce, argue, believe, claim, complain, deny, explain, insist, promise, propose, reply, request, say, state, suggest, think, warn: The director claimed (that) they decided to make the changes two years ago.
  • reporting verb + someone + that — assure, inform, persuade, remind, tell: He assured the interviewer that the company would continue to operate in the future.
  • reporting verb + to-infinitive — agree, ask, claim, offer, promise, propose, refuse: He promised to do his best for the employees.
  • reporting verb + someone + to-infinitive — advise, ask, encourage, invite, persuade, remind, tell, urge, warn: They urged the employees to stay calm.
  • reporting verb + preposition + -ing / noun — argue about, complain about, apologise for, insist on, complain to: He complained about the terrible food. / The company apologised for causing redundancies. / The manager insisted on seeing the staff. / He complained to the manager.
  • reporting verb + someone + preposition + -ing / noun — accuse of, advise about, remind about, advise on, congratulate on, blame for, thank for: They accused the company of planning badly. / They reminded her about the meeting. / He advised me on the deal. / They thanked her for coming.
  • reporting verb + -ing / noun — accept, admit, deny, suggest: The director denied having financial problems. / The employees accepted the offer of early redundancy.
  • reporting verb + someone (+ noun) — offer, promise, refuse: He offered her a job.
  • Some of the same verbs can be used in different ways (e.g. admit, claim, offer, persuade): West claimed that they decided to make the changes two years ago. / West claimed to have decided to make the changes two years ago.

B3 — Time references and other changes

  • We sometimes need to change other words in reported speech if they are reported at a different time from the original words: this → that; today → that day; yesterday → the day before; now → then/straight away; here → there; tomorrow → the following day/next day; next week → the following week.
  • He said he would see me tomorrow. (reported on the same day)  /  He said he would see me the next day. (reported at a later date)
  • Sometimes the context requires pronouns to change: 'I like you.' → He said he liked her.

B4 — Reporting questions

  • To report questions with question words (who, what, where, why, when, how), we keep the question word and use statement word order: What has led to this situation? → The interviewer asked what had led to this situation.
  • We can use if or whether to report yes/no questions: Are these cuts a result of Angleside's poor performance? → I asked if/whether these cuts were the result of Angleside's poor performance over the past five years.
  • The reported question does not use a question mark: I asked Mr West how he was going to deal with the problem. (not how was he going…)
Grammar extra — other ways of reporting
In spoken English we can also use apparently, supposedly and seemingly; in written English we might use an impersonal reporting construction. For example: According to the radio programme, they are cutting 150 jobs.
